Structural Risk, Mapped

Most AI safety work asks what one model does: can someone misuse it, does it make mistakes, does it want the wrong thing. Structural risk is the fourth question. What does AI do to the world around it, even when nobody misuses it and nothing malfunctions? This map covers the main arguments and the levers people are pulling in response, with every claim linked to its source.

Structural risk is the fourth risk area in Shah et al. (2025), alongside misuse, mistakes and misalignment (areas they say are neither exclusive nor exhaustive): harm that arises from multi-agent dynamics, incentives and institutions, with no single actor or model at fault. This map covers gradual disempowerment, power concentration and race dynamics, then the governance levers (compute governance, export controls, compute thresholds, frontier safety frameworks and the EU GPAI Code, liability, international coordination), each tied to primary sources.

Most safety work checks one robot: can a bad person trick it, does it make mistakes, does it want the wrong thing. This map asks a different question. What happens to the whole town when every shop, school and office has robots, even if every robot behaves? Twelve short pages on what could go wrong, and what grown-ups are doing about it.

00 the map

Three arcs, one question

Google DeepMind's 2025 safety paper groups AI risk into four areas: misuse, mistakes, misalignment and structural risks. The first three are about a model. The fourth is about everything around it, which is why the answers here are rules, incentives and institutions, not training methods.
